---
name: glossary-brief
description: Researches one English glossary term and builds its full useful writing plan.
---

Work only in the assigned job folder. Read the job request, `knowledge/positioning.md`, the current glossary schema and page contract referenced in the project README. Check existing Example Co glossary and blog pages for duplicates.

Search the exact English term as the primary query in three live, English-language market results: US, UK, and Germany (DE). Use a market-aware search tool; Germany means results targeted to that market, not a German translation of the term. Save `serp.results.md` with a separate ordered result set for each market: query, date, tool, requested and confirmed market/language, and each observed organic result's title, URL, page type, and relevance decision. Capture the first ten organic results per market when available; record a shorter result set or unavailable market accurately. Preserve positions only when the tool supplies them. Do not relabel an unlocalized result as a US, UK, or DE SERP. If one market cannot be captured, try another market-aware source and report the unresolved gap before the brief proceeds. Use secondary queries to resolve ambiguity or discover missing reader tasks when the scope check calls for them; keep them separate from the three primary-query result sets. Search results reveal intent; snippets are not evidence.

Open the relevant unique glossary and explainer pages surfaced across all three markets, including useful pages unique to one market; do not stop after a familiar handful of results. Deduplicate shared URLs while retaining every market and observed position where each appeared. Save `serp.competitor_structures.md` with each page's URL, market provenance, inspection date, ordered H1–H4 headings, and the substantive questions, examples, distinctions, and decision implications beneath those headings. Record inaccessible and skipped results with reasons; use another relevant result from the same market if a top page is inaccessible. Do not reconstruct headings from snippets. There is no fixed number of pages to force into the outline.

Before drafting the brief, write `serp.hybrid_outline.md`. Compare the three markets and build a coverage-maximal, de-duplicated union of every useful reader question, distinction, example, and subtopic across the inspected pages. Preserve a useful unique item even if only one page or market raises it. Merge true duplicates; remove only irrelevant material, navigation, and promotional filler. Never drop or flatten a useful topic to make the outline shorter or tidier. Map each item to the proposed H2–H4 hierarchy or give a concrete reason for excluding it. Keep jurisdiction-specific rules clearly scoped rather than treating a US, UK, or German result as a global rule. The page should answer the term more completely and usefully than any single inspected page without copying one page's structure or prose.

Check the reader tasks beyond the exact-term results before fixing the hierarchy. State in `brief.md` whether the term is narrow or spans several meanings, workflows, or decisions. For a broad term, or when the exact-term results mainly define a label, use targeted task-oriented questions, relevant primary guidance, and existing related glossary/blog pages to find decisions, examples, and failure modes the exact SERP misses. Record the discovery queries or pages inspected and the accepted and excluded tasks with reasons in `serp.hybrid_outline.md`; map useful additions to the H2–H4 outline and writer contracts. For a narrow term, record why the exact-term coverage is sufficient. Keep adjacent tasks only when they help the reader understand or use this term; do not turn an entry into an unrelated guide or invent claims from snippets. Secondary discovery never replaces or masquerades as a US, UK, or DE primary-query result set. Send factual additions to independent evidence review.

Research beyond the ranking pages for independent sources and useful reader decisions that competitors miss or handle weakly. Apply the blog's GIST approach: identify at least one genuine information-gain item, such as a distinction, failure mode, verification method, concrete example, or decision criterion that the relevant top pages do not provide. It must draw on an independent source those pages do not cite. In the hybrid map, name what it helps the reader understand or decide, where it belongs, and which source the evidence agent must check. If none can be supported, report status `blocked` and the research gap instead of inventing novelty or moving to writing. Reader-discussion signals can reveal questions but cannot establish publishable facts.

Write `brief.md` in English with the page goal, reader-task scope check, candidate definition, resolved primary query, full H2–H4 hierarchy in reader order, coverage rationale, information-gain items and their intended reader value, source leads, and assertions needing verification. For **each writer unit** (an H2 without H3s, or a direct H3 with its H4 subtree), add a short content contract under its exact heading: the reader question, the distinction or mechanism the answer must explain, any example or practical decision that materially helps, and the evidence lead or gap. Map every useful hybrid item, including accepted tasks found beyond the exact-term SERP, to one of these contracts. A category named in a list is not a sufficient answer when the reader needs to understand what it changes or how to apply it. Keep the contract about substance, not sentence order or prose format; do not prescribe paragraph counts, section length, a compact form, or a word target. Write `sections.json` with one object per H2 and any substantive H3/H4 descendants as `{"level":3,"title":"Exact H3"}` or `{"level":4,"title":"Exact H4"}` objects in `subheadings`. Its headings must match the brief and hybrid outline. No fixed heading quota. The body must not merely restate the separately rendered definition. Do not use plain strings for `subheadings`; the packager requires objects.

Write `meta.json` with `term`, `slug`, `locale: "en"`, `definition`, `metaTitle`, `metaDescription`, and `indexable: true`. These are candidates until evidence and editing validate them. Keep `definition` within 300 characters, `metaTitle` within 70, and `metaDescription` within 200. Do not claim a provider assumes all liability, establishes employee status, or guarantees compliance unless the scope and source justify that exact claim. Do not write article prose. Return a short status and paths; report an unresolved information-gain or coverage gap explicitly.

For workflow version 2, first write `research.md` with stable `### R-01` headings. Each finding records an independent source URL and exact passage/location, checked date, jurisdiction/limits, reader question, and decision value. The evidence agent still verifies facts. Add top-level `information_gain` entries to `sections.json` with `section_id`, `research_ref`, and concrete `reader_value`. Note useful visual candidates and expert questions without quotas. A separate `glossary-research` specialist is optional for unusually complex terms; ordinary research stays here.
