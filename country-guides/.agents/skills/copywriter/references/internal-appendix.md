## A.1 Risk map

| Financial | Legal | Corporate | Operational |
|---|---|---|---|
| An invoice paid twice because it arrived by email and by chat · an invoice paid against an expired statement of work · back taxes and penalties when an engagement is re-examined and no agreement can be produced · a month-end close held up by invoices nobody approved · budget overruns noticed only after payment | No signed agreement on file for an active contractor · the signed version is not the current version · no proof of who signed and when · no clause on ownership of the work result · a dispute with a contractor over scope that the paperwork cannot settle | Due diligence stalls on the contractor file · an auditor qualifies the accounts for undocumented spend · an acquirer discounts the price for missing agreements · an enterprise client's vendor review asks for the contractor set and gets a folder of screenshots · a bank asks for the document behind a payment | Documents scattered across mailboxes and shared folders · two versions of one agreement in circulation · approvals given in a chat and lost · a manager leaves and their approvals leave with them · no history of changes |
| reduced by approvals and the document trail | reduced by agreements from templates and e-signature | reduced by the document trail and exports | reduced by one record per contractor |

## A.2 «Yes, you can do it that way — and here is what may happen»

| Risk | Consequence |
|---|---|
| No current signed agreement | the engagement cannot be evidenced when someone asks |
| Approval in a chat | nobody can show who agreed to the amount |
| Invoices in free format | manual retyping and duplicated payments |
| Ownership of the work result | the company cannot show it owns what it paid for |
| Month-end close | the books stay open until the last invoice is found |
| A contractor in a new country | the usual template misses a local requirement |
| Due diligence / acquisition | the deal slows down or the price is discounted |
| Enterprise vendor review | the contract with the large client is delayed |
| A manager's departure | their approvals and context are gone |
| Reputation | contractors whose invoices sit unapproved talk to each other |

## A.3 The understanding gap (details for content strategy)

- FMS — freelancer management system, AOR — agent of record, EOR — employer of record: the audience confuses the three terms.
- Up to ~10 contractors: «a spreadsheet is fine» — explain what a complete contractor record looks like and what comes next; we do not sell here, we teach.
- 10+ (first finance hire): approvals start to queue in personal mailboxes — approval routes become the entry point.
- 50+ (head of finance in place, the founder no longer decides alone): exports, roles and the document trail matter most.
- Clients often already sign agreements by email and keep invoices in a shared folder; they do not know the difference between FMS, AOR and EOR and do not see the risk of undocumented approvals; many take Example Co for an HR platform, which it is not.

## A.5 Objection handling

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| Globex / Initech / Hooli / Soylent also keep agreements and invoices in order? | They do it well, but they are built around employment and HR, and contractor paperwork is one module among many. We were built around the contractor record from the start — we know that work better and are more flexible on approval routes. |
| They are the market standard and you are not? | The standard of the broad market is not always the best fit for one job. If you need the whole employment and HR stack, the choice is obvious. If you need contractor agreements, invoices and approvals in one place without the rest — that is us. |
| Why do you not have feature X that Y has? | We deliberately do not build a universal HR platform. We concentrate on what affects the daily work of a finance and operations team with contractors in several countries. |
| Northwind / Contoso / Fabrikam / Umbrella / Vandelay are cheaper! | If the only criterion is the lowest price, there are cheaper options. If it matters that every invoice has a signed agreement and a named approver behind it, compare the document trail and the approval routes, not only the price. |
| What makes you better? | We do one thing: the contractor record — agreement, invoices, approvals — kept so that it can be handed to an accountant, an auditor or an investor as it is. We do not try to replace the HR stack or the accounting system. |
