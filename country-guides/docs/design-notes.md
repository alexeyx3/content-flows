# Design notes: why the country-guides flow works the way it does

The rules themselves live in `.agents/skills/country-guide/SKILL.md`, `AGENTS.md`,
`knowledge/guides-rules.md`, `knowledge/facts-schema.md` and the role files under
`agents-src/agents/`. This file records the reason behind each rule, so that someone adapting the
flow knows what a rule protects before changing it. Add a row here whenever you change the flow.

## Structure of the flow

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| One country is one job, and the three pages are produced together. | The subpages rely on the country page's figures, and a mismatch of figures between the three pages is the main risk of the section. In the search results one site's two pages about the same country gave different employer-cost figures and had been updated more than a year apart; the flow is built so that this cannot happen. |
| Each page is a folder in the blog's job format, and the blog scripts the flow needs are copied under `vendor/blog/`. | Rendering, uploads, packaging, the QA gate and review documents are reused instead of rewritten, and a copy keeps the flow working when the blog project changes. |
| Every independent, dependency-ready specialist scope starts at once; there is no three-specialist cap. The project allows 32 threads; a lower live limit is recorded from the actual rejection. | Idle scopes add elapsed time without adding quality. A configuration value does not prove that the running session adopted it, so only a real rejection shows the effective limit. |
| `changes` and `practitioners` research start right after the fact pack; `angle` waits for the search results; visual QA runs in parallel with writing. | These lines need different inputs. Starting each one as soon as its inputs exist keeps the slow SERP capture off the critical path. |
| Maintenance of the pipeline never resumes existing jobs, resets their queue status, reruns publication or rewrites job artifacts; an authorization given for another workflow does not cover this flow's writes. | Existing jobs carry review documents and CMS IDs accepted under their own contract. A maintenance pass that "refreshed" them could overwrite reviewed text or write to the CMS without a request. |
| After the first three countries of a new setup, the market measurement and the lessons are written into the flow document and the rules. | One country tests the content, not the template. The rules should change once, from measured results, before the template is repeated across many countries. |

## Fact pack and calculations

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| The fact pack is built first. Every number on the three pages comes only from `facts.json` (F-), `derived.json` (C-) or evidence (E-, V-); a number found elsewhere is a fabrication, and a gap is reported, never filled. | Data first, text second is the only way to keep one figure in one place across three pages. During an early build one contribution total appeared with two decimals in a table, rounded to one decimal in a chart and as an approximate figure in the header, all in one file; spread across three pages of prose, nothing would have caught such a pair. |
| The fact pack is split into two scopes, `employment` and `contractors`, built by two agents in parallel with separate ID ranges (F-001…F-499, F-500…F-999) and merged by `check_facts.py --merge`. | The two scopes draw on different laws and authorities, so they can be researched at the same time; separate ID ranges let the parts merge without collisions. |
| Every value carries the exact supporting passage, the URL of the page that carries the figure, the publisher, the document's actual title, a tier and an ISO access date. A value that cannot be confirmed goes to `unconfirmed` and is never written. | Every figure in this section expires: minimum wages and contribution ceilings change every year. A page without sources and dates is wrong a year later and the reader has no way to tell. Among competing guide pages only a small minority showed an update date or a source per figure, so this is also where a new page can be more trustworthy than the top. |
| The exact quote is taken from the raw page text, not from a page-reading tool's summary. A quote that could not be read raw is marked `[summary]`, reaches the page evidence as `partial`, and needs an independently supported row before publication; it is never promoted silently. | A summary is a model's paraphrase of the page and can mix up figures; it cannot prove that the source says what the claim says. |
| Source priority: law and official gazette → ministry, tax authority, social insurance, labour inspectorate, central bank, statistics office → Big Four and law firms → vendors only about themselves. Content farms, third-party "best EOR" rankings, AI-generated aggregators and competitors' country guides are leads, never sources. Local-language official pages are preferred over English summaries. | Within this niche figures circulate from one vendor guide to another: one competing guide cited another vendor's guide as the source of its table. Only the original stops a copied error. |
| Announced changes (next year's minimum wage, new thresholds) are recorded as their own facts with effective dates. | A dated change is a fact a reader can act on; mixing it into the current value would make one of the two wrong. |
| An optional research memory (`raw/hint-*.yaml`, vendor cards under `companies/`) is a lead only: every figure is reopened at the source, and its wording is never copied. | A stored fact may be stale, and copied wording would make the guides share sentences with every other text built from the same memory. |
| The eight header figures are short (at most 32 characters, no commentary), `employerContributions` equals the sum of the `in_header` employer rates, and the figures appear in the country page body character for character; the routes table uses the same rate and notice period. | The header is a set of CMS fields the site renders above the body, and the body is prose. Only a literal, script-checked match keeps the field and the text from drifting apart. |
| A country whose employer levies have no truthful single wage percentage uses the fixed-levy model: `value: null`, `model: fixed`, a short evidenced `display` label. Never `0%`, never an invented rate. Review can proceed; CMS export stays blocked until the header can display it. | A zero or a wage-equivalent rate would be false. The CMS stores this figure as a number, so the truthful label can be reviewed but not yet published. |
| Calculations are done by `calc.py` from the fact pack and `calc_spec.json`, never by a model; the orchestrator checks every formula (caps, floors, which share applies) by hand. | The employer's cost of a salary is the figure most often wrong on competing pages and the base of the cost charts. The script removes arithmetic and rounding slips; it cannot detect a wrong model of the law, so the formula needs a human check. |
| Monthly thresholds are never silently annualised or derived from annual ones; annual totals need sourced annual caps; rounding modes and decimals follow a sourced rule or are stated as a budget convention. | Converting a threshold between periods produces a plausible total that no source supports. |
| A fact later found wrong is marked `superseded` and kept; restricted or unconfirmed facts stay with their provenance; a calculation inherits the restriction of its inputs. | The audit trail shows what changed and why, and arithmetic does not make an unverified input legally applicable. |

## Search results and research

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Search results are captured for three page clusters (10 queries) in four markets: US, two further English-language markets (GB, AU) and the country itself. US and the country are always collected. | A single market hides useful blocks. The extra markets bring other competing pages, PAA questions and structures into the inventory. `serp/geo-overlap.md` measures the share of top-10 URLs from the extra markets that are absent from the US and country results, and how many useful blocks they added; the set of markets is decided by that measurement after the first three countries. |
| Empty query × market pairs are retried once (`serp.py --refill`). | A silently empty market shrinks the inventory the brief is built from, and the brief would never know. |
| Pages in the results that look like vendor rankings are written out separately (`serp/rankings.md`): who, how many entries, which criteria. | The contractors ranking must be broader than any ranking in the country's results; this file is what it is measured against. |
| Keyword data is optional. A missing tool is recorded and the work continues; keyword volumes are never invented. | Search data informs the outline but does not gate it; an invented volume would steer the brief on a number nobody measured. |
| Research runs three lines beyond the fact pack and the results: `changes`, `practitioners`, `angle`. Primary sources and own calculations are not separate lines: the fact pack and the calculator cover them. | Each line answers a question the top does not: what changed, what people who do this work say, and what the top gets wrong or leaves out. |
| `changes`: what changed recently and what is announced, each with its effective date; anything effective within the next six months is flagged for an advisory note. «Recent changes» is a core section on the country page. | Keyword data showed demand for news about a country's labour law, and among competing pages only one carried a dated list of recent changes. It is the direct answer to "is this still current?". |
| `practitioners`: verbatim words (at most 25) from local law firms, tax advisers, chambers, associations and officials, with name, role, organisation, date, URL and location in the source. Forum threads give pains, which are findings, not quotes. | Practitioners explain decisions and trade-offs that statutes do not. A paraphrase or a snippet is not their words. |
| `angle`: the figures of 6–10 top results are compared with the fact pack in an internal «Top audit» that is never published; the line also finds the country's unique blocks and proposes calculations by F- IDs for the script. | The audit tells the brief which correct figures to make prominent. Naming a competitor as wrong on the company's own site is close to disparagement, so the page states the correct figure with its source instead. |
| Every page carries at least one unique block that no neighbouring country page has: a regime, threshold, registry, recent law or calculation. The brief names it. | With one template across many countries, identical skeletons are a redundancy the site creates for itself. In the results, a large vendor's contractor pages were the same template with the country name swapped, carried no facts about the country, and drew almost no traffic. |

## Briefs and page structure

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| The brief is a hybrid: the fixed core of guides-rules 3.1 in its order, every useful block the top carries in any market, and blocks nobody has. The result must be more complete than any single page in the results; a skipped top block needs a written reason. | A union of the top's blocks without a new node gives a replaceable page, and each competing page carried a different subset. When structures were inventoried across several dozen pages, the hybrid ended with more blocks than any of them. Blocks that describe a vendor's own product (its insurance plan line by line, its device management) are skipped: they are not facts about the country. |
| A brief without information gain beyond the top, or with a core section the fact pack cannot support, is blocked. | Blocking forces research instead of filler; an unsupported core section would make the writer invent. |
| The core sections stay on every page, even though every competitor covers them. | These blocks cannot win on their own, but a page that drops the basics for originality loses in another way. |
| Each page opens with a direct answer of 2–3 sentences without a heading, and every section opens with its own answer in one plain sentence. | AI Overviews appeared on almost every business query in this topic; a direct answer serves them and the reader who reads only the first paragraph. |
| The country page keeps one section per fact topic (minimum wage, taxes and contributions, working time, leave, termination). | Traffic in this section comes from single-fact queries such as a country's minimum wage or payroll; a fact that has its own heading can answer its own query. |
| `sections.json` gives every H2 a content contract: the reader's question, what to explain, which F-/C-/R- IDs to use, the table format, the visual marker, the quote and the links. There is no word quota. Outline and instructions are reconciled before delivery. | The writer works from the instruction, not from the outline. A requirement left only in the outline is not reconstructed from a shorter instruction. |
| Subpages take figures only from the fact pack. They may revisit a country-page topic when their model changes the answer, written from scratch for that model, with a link to the country page. | One figure in one place; and a topic the reader needs on the subpage is better answered there than sent away. |
| Every page ends with its own «Sources» section, with the FAQ right before it; additions never go between FAQ and Sources. | The original editor guide gave Sources only to the country page; every page states figures and needs its own list. |
| Sources entries are `Publisher. [Document title](exact page). Accessed <date>.` in order of first visible use, renumbered by `order_sources.py` and audited by hand before QA. | Bare URLs used as link text do not wrap: in an early review they made the whole page wider than a phone screen. The sorter cannot see compound table citations or relative links, so its PASS alone is not the audit. |
| The access date is the fact's or evidence row's date, never today's; prose carries no freshness markers such as «As of <month>». | The access date states when the figure was read. The site shows the page's own update date. |
| Checklists use ordinary bullets, never task-list tokens. | The CMS path renders `- [ ]` as literal brackets. |
| Prose uses `employer of record` or `EOR` consistently; headings name the country's actual question rather than a template phrase. | An early review found the term written two ways on one page. Template headings repeated across countries are the same redundancy the unique block fights. |

## Ranking on the contractors page

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| The ranking exists on the contractors page only; there is none on the EOR page. | Example Co is not an employer of record. Its own ranking of EOR providers without an EOR product would not be honest, and with one it would be self-promotion. |
| The ranking is broader than any ranking in the country's results, never a padded top-10; every entry is a genuine way to engage contractors in that country; banks and payment services are not entries. | A "top N, we are first" list is the most replaceable kind of block: any vendor writes it about itself. It becomes a reference only when it covers more real options than anything in the results. |
| The columns are what changes by country: model, presence in the country, contracts under local law, IP assignment, local documents, classification support. All seven columns stay separate. | Ranking by verifiable country criteria makes the table a country fact; general claims such as "we operate there" do not pass that test. |
| Example Co is first, and whatever aspect the ranking describes for Example Co is filled for every vendor from its own pages or marked «not published». No column or note exists only for Example Co; its entry is not longer or softer than the others. | A column that only the own product has, or that every vendor has except the own product, looks odd and reads as an advert. Banning an aspect from the ranking because the own product cannot state it would hide information the reader needs; equal terms keep the aspect and mark gaps «not published» for anyone. |
| Each vendor note is specific to the country; a note that could move to another country unchanged is rewritten. | A swapped-country note is the template problem again, in miniature. |
| Vendor facts come from the vendor's own pages, with date, as V- rows. A negative claim needs a product page, a pricing page or an independent review, never a slogan or the absence of a mention. | A missing mention does not prove a missing feature, and a wrong negative claim about a named vendor is the most damaging error the table can contain. |
| Liability is never stated as fact: no vendor or arrangement "takes on liability", "indemnifies" or "guarantees compliance". The page says who the contracting party is; whether liability moves depends on the contract's indemnification clause. | The page cannot prove an outcome that is decided by a contract it has not read. |
| Zero outbound links to competitors; their pages appear in Sources as plain text with host and path. | Readers can still check the source, and the page does not send traffic to pages it competes with. |

## Visuals

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| At least two or three visuals per page wherever verified data exists, no ceiling. A visual may duplicate a table, and the table always stays. | Search engines and AI assistants do not read text inside images: the table carries the figures, the chart shows the relation. |
| Every value and node cites F-/C-/E- IDs present in that page's evidence; calculated values cite C- rows with the formula in the methodology; captions and alt text describe the subject, never the research. | A chart is a claim like any sentence and needs the same evidence. |
| Visuals are planned after evidence and rendered before the writer starts; the writer places each marker once and says what the reader should take from it. | The writer can refer to an image only if it exists and its data has been verified. |
| An independent visual QA opens every rendered PNG at full size and at 390 px before any upload and records a verdict per image with its hash. | A chart can match its JSON and still be wrong as an image: a truncated axis, overlapping labels, an arrow drawn through a label. Small text that reads well at full size can become illegible at phone width. |
| Labels are short because the renderer refuses to truncate; in a decision tree with three children the middle edge stays unlabelled. | The renderer draws the connector through the middle edge's label. |
| The same chart is not repeated on two pages of a country. | A subpage chart answers its own model; a repeated image is a cross-page repeat. |
| Captions use compact numbered references to the page's Sources; after renumbering, `package_article.py --bind-visuals` refreshes them before the style report hash and the QA seal. | Long bibliographies under each image clutter the page, and stale numbers would point to the wrong source. |

## Writing, editing and the style pass

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| The writer writes exactly one H2 with its H3/H4 per request, also through the API; the opening is its own request. Sections are never batched to save calls. | One section per request preserves each section's depth, completeness and necessary length; batching to reduce calls was considered and rejected. |
| Every writer request carries the full country packet, the page brief, all previous sections and the full merged running state (canonical terms, facts used, underlying sources, covered points, links placed). | The state keeps sections from repeating facts, definitions and transitions, and the source map keeps the Sources list complete when a claim has no inline link. |
| The country page is drafted first; the subpages receive its text, and the type and hash of that context are recorded before the first subpage request. | Subpages link to the country page and write overlapping topics for their own model; the hash shows which version they saw. |
| A figure the instruction asks for but the evidence lacks is written around and reported, never filled from memory or the web. | An unsupported number that reads well is the error the whole flow is built to prevent. |
| The editor keeps every fact, header figure, visual marker and quote; any cut evidenced fact is listed with its reason. Rework uses patch mode on the named sections only. | An editor who shortens around a gap produces a thinner page that looks finished, and QA cannot see what was lost unless it is recorded. |
| A separate whole-page style pass by the same editor runs after images are bound and before final QA, and saves `humanization_report.md` with concrete before/after examples and the SHA-256 of the reviewed text. Any later edit repeats the affected pass. | The first edit happens before images are bound and never sees the finished page. The hash stops a later change from riding on an old report. The editorial AI-Score is a diagnostic, not a detector result, and never replaces independent QA. |
| Editor, style pass and QA judge the whole-page reading: paragraph continuity, link clutter, fragment-like blocks, repeated card patterns. | Clean individual sentences do not make a page ready to publish; a page assembled section by section can pass sentence-level checks and still read as fragments. |
| Paragraphs follow one coherent thought, normally 2–4 sentences; there is no word ceiling, and a machine word-count warning is a prompt to review, not a demand to split. | Splitting a rule away from its conditions and consequence to meet a word count damages the meaning. |
| Links serve the reader: the opening links to the country's own subpages; later sections link a registry, form or specific provision when opening it helps; other originals stay in Sources. | Evidence lives in Sources and the evidence files; turning every fact into an anchor clutters the page without making it more verifiable. |

## QA, repeats, receipts and review

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| `check_guide.py` runs before QA: one H1, heading depth, core sections and their order, FAQ before Sources, Sources format and completeness, competitor links, internal links outside the site map, banned topics, stop-listed brands, forbidden claims next to the brand, the brand's spelling, header figures in the body, verbatim repeats. | Deterministic checks are cheaper and more reliable as scripts; QA spends its judgement on what a script cannot decide. |
| Repeats are measured across the three pages of the country, all finished countries and the project's vendor cards, Sources excluded: a shared run of 20 or more words is a warning for QA to judge, 45 or more blocks. | Overlap between the three pages and with neighbouring countries is acceptable, since the same topic is written for another model. Only large verbatim pieces are copying: shared skeleton yes, shared sentences no. |
| QA is an independent native agent with ten gates; it re-checks the 6–8 highest-risk figures live, judges every repeat warning, and inspects the actual images. Re-checks go to the same QA thread. | The agent that edited a page cannot give an independent verdict on it, and a verdict from JSON alone is not an inspection of the image. |
| A missing or stale style report blocks final QA. | QA must be able to tell which text the style pass actually reviewed. |
| After a QA PASS the text is frozen: any change means repackaging, repeating the affected QA and sealing again (`workflow_gate.py seal-qa`). | The receipt binds the verdict to the exact text; a later edit would otherwise ride on an old verdict. |
| Review approval is recorded only after the publisher actually approves (`approve-review --by --reference`); the flow never generates an approval. | The approval is the human checkpoint before publication; an automatic receipt would remove it. |
| Practitioner quotes go through evidence as E- rows; `expert_quotes.json` stays empty by design. No comments on behalf of the company's own staff. | The shared quote validator admits only the article's author and editor to the quote manifest, while guide quotes come from external people and need their own verified row. |
| A quote in the local language may be used in English translation, marked as translated; the evidence row keeps the original words. | The translation must not add or soften anything, and the original lets a reviewer check that. |

## CMS and publication

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Before a country is selected, `check_cms_country.py` verifies its actual ISO code and the exporter's fields against the live CMS schema, and `new_guide_job.py` repeats the check before creating any folder or changing the queue. An unsupported code or an unavailable check blocks the country; the CMS and the site are not changed, and no other code is substituted. | The CMS stores the country code as an enumerated field, and the site derives the flag and the region from it. A code missing from the running schema cannot be saved, and only a live read shows what the CMS accepts. Finding that out after research and paid drafting wastes the whole job; a substituted code would show the wrong flag and region. |
| Media upload, CMS writes and indexing run only after an explicit request in the conversation; an authorization persists within that conversation; credentials grant no rights. | Publication is the one irreversible step, and the flow must never infer it. |
| `strapi_guides.py` updates existing records instead of creating duplicates, runs the export gate first, and reads the public record back: the sanitized body must equal what was sent. The body must pass a copy of the CMS sanitizer without losses. | A second subpage of the same kind would give two records for one address. The CMS sanitizer removes attributes and tags it does not allow, so only a read-back proves the published page is the reviewed one. |
| Every published guide page is indexable: an authorized publication writes `indexable: true`; a noindex preview is written only on an explicit request, and an update never silently resets a live guide to noindex. | The section exists to answer search demand, and a published page closed to indexing is invisible. A flag written from a default during an update could de-index a live guide without anyone noticing. |

## Models, writer backend and cost

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Reasoning effort is `medium` for every specialist, including difficult legal and calculation work, and is passed explicitly on every spawn. Only the coordinator runs at `high`. | Specialists work from a verified packet under tight file contracts with scripts behind them; difficult figures are handled by the calculator and evidence rows, not by more reasoning. Passing the value explicitly stops a specialist from inheriting the coordinator's setting. The coordinator holds the long context and the routing decisions. |
| Research and brief roles stay on the strong model line; moving them to the mid line needs the publisher's approval. | Judgement about sources and about what a reader needs propagates into every later stage. |
| Drafting runs on a mid model through OpenRouter; editing, the style pass and final QA run natively on the strong line. | Drafting from a verified packet under a tight contract is the stage where a cheaper model holds quality; judgement stages are not. |
| The provider is pinned; each response's model version, effort, provider and the reliability of `usage.cost` are checked before the result is applied. A mismatch or an API error stops the stage; there is no automatic fallback to another provider, model or backend. | Quality and cost were measured on one route. A silent substitution invalidates both, and silent provider or price changes are the usual way API spend drifts. |
| Native stages are verified against the actual provider rollout with `verify_model_route.py` after dispatch and at completion; receipts go to `metrics/`. | A configuration file or an agent's self-reported model is not proof of what ran, and an already-open chat can keep its old model after a configuration edit. |
| The three pages receive the full shared fact and evidence packet; it is not split or filtered per page. | Splitting the packet per page was considered as a cost saving and deliberately not adopted: the subpages rely on the country's facts and restrictions, and a mismatch between pages is the main risk. |
| An explicit 30-minute cache boundary sits after the complete stable packet; image addresses and source numbering in their export-dependent form are kept out of it. | Every section request repeats the same large packet. Parts that change with each export would break the cached prefix. |
| The writer returns the new section and append-only state additions; the code merges them and sends the full merged state to the next call. | A later call cannot drop or rewrite earlier state, and every call still sees all of it. |
| Each revision has a total budget and a per-call reserve checked before every request, with at most two workers. Requests are not retried automatically; an ambiguous result is resolved by finding the call's status and charge first. A changed input packet starts a new revision; older revisions and receipts stay frozen. | An interrupted request may already have been charged. A rerun reuses stored receipts, so nothing is paid twice, and frozen revisions keep past costs and outputs auditable. |
| An upstream rate-limit rejection without `usage.cost` is reconciled against the key's accumulated spend (`reconcile_openrouter_429.py`); unknown timeouts and partial outputs remain blockers. | Until reconciled, such a charge is unknown, and an unknown charge must not be counted as zero. |
| For every country, actual charges are reported per page and across all revisions, retries included, with attempts of unknown charge counted separately; reserves and subscription observations are reported apart. | A reserve is a ceiling, not a charge, and a total that drops unknown attempts understates spend. |
| The flow's OpenRouter key is labelled for this flow; a generic key is refused. | Spend can then be tracked and limited per flow. |
| The coordinator appends sanitized account-limit snapshots to `metrics/usage-snapshots.jsonl` before a batch, after research, after drafting and style, after final QA and delivery, and on resume. Missing values are null, never zero; no account IDs or credentials are stored; only matching limit windows are compared. | Subscription usage is visible only as an account-wide percentage that other chats also move. Snapshots at fixed checkpoints are the only comparable record, and they are observations, not exact per-job costs. |
| Measured experiments (`openai_guide_experiment.py`, `openrouter_experiment.py`) never change production routes and never authorize publication; a different execution is adopted only after comparing frozen inputs, with final QA kept independent. | A trial run that quietly became the production route would bypass the quality comparison it was meant to provide. |
