# Glossary: words to avoid / replace — Example Co (current edition)

Source: the company's editorial policy and positioning document — its vocabulary ("use / do not use") and its list of what is forbidden. Owner: Jane Doe.

## Gradient: "Never" vs "Caution" vs "✅ Primary"

**Never** = do not use in any marketing, UI, core blocks, value proposition, FAQ, or technical documentation — no exceptions unless noted.
**Caution** = allowed in technical documentation or FAQ only — not in hero blocks, H1, main CTAs, or value prop.
**✅ Primary** = flagship/primary term, free to use anywhere including H1 (the opposite of a restriction).

## Forbidden without prior Legal & Compliance approval

No creative, slide, message, or brief may contain the following without prior Legal & Compliance sign-off:

- **Staffing frame:** staffing agency, recruiting, talent sourcing, matching, vetted talent, bench.
- **Employment frame:** employer of record, employer, HR system, benefits, hiring on the client's behalf.
- **Legal services:** law firm, legal adviser, legal advice, legal opinion, "reviewed by our lawyers", conclusions about a specific jurisdiction or case.
- **Money movement:** payment provider, payment platform, «pay contractors through Example Co».
- **Legal certification:** certified, notarised, «legally verified» agreements; a seal or stamp next to e-signature.
- **Speed:** instant, same-day, in minutes, real-time approval.
- **Legal/compliance guarantees:** audit-proof, fully compliant, risk-free, "misclassification protection", classification checks, "we guarantee".
- **Superlatives and numbers:** best, leading, number one; customer counts, growth, retention or satisfaction figures that the site does not publish.
- **Cost:** any price wording other than the verbatim line from the pricing page; cheap, free, low-cost; discounts that are not on the pricing page.
- **Geo-specifics:** no lists of regions or countries — only the generalized "contractors in 60+ countries", once per page.
- **Category labels:** staffing agency, marketplace, employer of record, law firm, payment provider, HR system; "Agent of Record" as a name for the product.
- **Other:** roadmap dates and unreleased features; named customers without a published case study.

**One-line rule:** if a formulation isn't in this policy, it can't go into public communication without approval.

## Dictionary-translator: internal meaning → allowed public phrasing

| Internal meaning (not for the public) | Allowed public phrasing |
|---|---|
| finance signs off every invoice before anyone pays it | invoices are approved in one place; the company pays through its own bank or payment service |
| cheaper than an employer of record | the verbatim price line; no comparisons |
| works everywhere / a list of regions | "contractors in 60+ countries" (no geography) |
| legally safe agreements | agreements from templates, signed with e-signature |
| audit-ready / passes due diligence | a document trail per contractor; helps keep contractor documents in order |
| fast approvals | do not use; at most "in a few steps" |
| N customers / growing X% a quarter | do not use unless the figure is on the site |
| upcoming integration / planned feature | do not use |
| we replace your lawyer | templates your own counsel can adapt |
| we act as the agent of record | do not use; Example Co is software, the arrangement stays between the company and the contractor |
| background checks / identity verification / vetting | collecting the contractor's details and a signed agreement before work starts |

When choosing between two formulations, pick the one further from "we supply people, money or legal cover" and closer to "software that keeps agreements, invoices and approvals in one place."

## Decision rule when in doubt

1. Does it sound like Example Co supplies people, moves money or gives legal advice? → Don't use it.
2. Does it sound like software that keeps agreements, invoices and approvals in one place? → Use it.
3. Does it match what the product does (template / signature / invoice / approval / export)? → Use it.
4. Does it create employer-employee framing? → Don't use it.
5. Does it promise an outcome (compliance, an audit result, speed)? → Replace with "helps keep contractor documents in order" or name the concrete step.
6. Does it describe Example Co doing the action, or the client? → The client's team signs, approves and exports; Example Co is the software they do it in.
7. Is it geo-specific (a named country/region) or a direct competitor comparison/price claim? → Generalize, or move it to internal-only material (see `references/internal-appendix.md`).
