## Rules (verbatim-critical)

### Naming & category
- Public brand name: ALWAYS «Example Co» (two words, both capitalised) in any reader-facing text,

  forms are internal shorthand only.)
- **Category formula (approved by the publisher, as it stands on the site):**
  «software for companies that work with independent contractors abroad: agreements, invoices and
  approvals in one place». The full formula appears ONCE per page — in the Example Co card or

  paperwork, work with contractors abroad. For another publishing language the approved translation

- **Labels never used for Example Co:** «freelancer management system», «contractor
  compliance platform», «global workforce platform», «vendor onboarding tool». «Freelancer

  that compare operating models), never Example Co's own label.
- **Product name is ONLY «Example Workspace»**, capitalised. FMS / AOR / EOR are industry terms —

- **«Agent of Record»** is not an Example Co product name or label. It MAY describe how an

- **Describe Example Co from the client's side.** Companies keep contractor agreements and approve
  contractor invoices in Example Workspace. Never «Example Co works with contractors in X» or
  «Example Co's contractors» — it reads as if the contractors were Example Co's own. Avoid
  tautologies such as «Example Workspace is a workspace for working with contractors».
- **Engagement words are allowed next to Example Co:** work with, engage, contractor, independent
  contractor, agreement, statement of work. Do not present Example Co as the party that engages the
  contractor, do not call the client's relationship with a contractor employment, and do not use
  «hire», «staff», «workforce» or «payroll» as its product or category words.
- **Editorial positioning: the practical-guide company.** Explain how contractor paperwork works —
  what an agreement has to contain, what makes an invoice approvable, what a checker asks for —
  and surface risks carefully through published cases and official guidance (court rulings on
  worker status, IRS, HMRC and DOL tests) — never as scare slogans.
  Semantics: «in order», «documented», «on record». The word «compliant» is NOT used as a promise
  about the client's position; it may appear only when quoting a law, a regulator or a source.
- One-line product truth: every contractor has one record — the signed agreement, the invoices and
  who approved each of them. The client runs the work; drafting from templates, signing, invoice
  collection, sign-off and the export to accounting stay inside one workspace.

### §2 Three public value pillars (all three pass the legal filter)
1. **Agreements** — the strongest and cleanest public asset. Every contractor is engaged through an
   agreement built from a template and signed with an e-signature; the signed version is stored
   against the contractor. When a statement of work changes, the new version is signed and the old
   one stays in the history. Before a renewal, a new project or a check, the list shows per
   contractor whether a current signed agreement exists and where it is missing or about to
   expire — gaps are visible in advance, not when someone asks.
2. **Document trail and readiness for a check** — one record per contractor instead of mailboxes,
   shared folders and spreadsheets: agreement, statements of work, every invoice, who approved it
   and when. The set can be handed to an auditor, an investor or an acquirer as it is, and the
   export to accounting software carries the same invoice references as the trail.
3. **Approvals and saved manual work** — this is where savings MAY be discussed, but only savings
   of TIME and MANUAL WORK, never of money. Invoices arrive in one format, go to the person who
   owns the budget, and are approved or returned with a reason; reminders replace chasing;
   role-based access; a named contact for the account. **Do not claim that Example Co runs
   background checks, identity verification or vetting of contractors.** Product rule:
   background checks and identity verification are not an Example Co product function; there is no
   «vetted contractors» badge, and no page may claim one.

- Not a payment provider, payment platform or financial service —

- Not a marketplace, not a law firm.
- Not a staffing agency, not a recruiter.
- **Not an EOR** — the client engages independent contractors under its own agreements; Example Co

- Not a freelancer marketplace — it does not source, vet or match contractors.
- Not a law firm or legal adviser — gives no legal or tax advice and claims no regulatory status.

## Notes / context

### Role-based entry points (product is one; the entry point adapts)
- **Founder** (in companies up to ~30 people also acts as head of finance and operations). Handles
  contractor paperwork personally, usually late in the evening; every outside question — from an
  investor, an accountant, a bank — ends up on their desk. Wants to stop being the place where
  agreements and invoices live. → «Every contractor's paperwork in one place, not in your inbox.»
- **Head of finance / controller** (appears at ~30–50 people; not an initiator but a FILTER —
  evaluates what operations or the founder brought in). Works with evidence before numbers.
  Professional reputation rests on two things: every payment the company makes has a document
  behind it, and declared processes work exactly as declared. Convinced not by promises but by what
  they can verify themselves. → every invoice has a signed agreement and a named approver; statuses
  are current, not reconstructed at month-end; the trail and its supporting documents are available
  for review; price published without small print; the export matches the ledger; the whole history
  can be produced for an auditor, a bank or an investor at any moment.
- **Operations / people-operations manager** — initiator and ambassador. Needs contractor
  engagements to be visible to management in agreements, dates and statuses after the company has
  chosen the person and agreed the work.
- **Team lead / budget owner** — approves the invoices. The bigger the team, the more approvals
  wait in a personal mailbox and the higher the cost of one missed. → «You approve the work. The
  paperwork follows it.»
- Scale axis: up to ~30 — the founder decides, speak the language of "get it out of my inbox";
  ~30–50 — the head of finance appears as a filter, add verifiability and proof points; 50+ —
  exports, roles and approval routes move to the front.

### Verticals (one product, industry-specific entry point)
Common wedge across all verticals: not "find and sign up people" but **"every contractor has a
signed agreement and every invoice an approval at the moment someone checks"** — at a client
handover, a funding round or an audit.
- **Software consultancies** — a client project is delivered by a mixed team of staff and
  contractors in several countries; the client's procurement asks for proof that everyone on the
  project works under a signed agreement. The gap surfaces at the kick-off call, when two people
  turn out to be working on an expired statement of work.
  → «Every contractor on the project — under a signed agreement before the kick-off.»
- **Translation and language-services companies** — dozens of linguists per language pair, many of them
  with one or two small invoices a month. Invoices arrive in every format a text editor can
  produce, and the coordinator retypes them. → «Many small invoices — one format, one queue.»
- **Market-research firms** — fieldwork partners and interviewers are engaged per study and per
  country; when the study closes, nobody is sure which invoices are still to come.
  → «The study is closed when the last invoice is approved, not when someone remembers.»
- **E-commerce brands** — photographers, copywriters and store managers abroad are engaged by
  marketing, not by finance; finance learns about a contractor from the first invoice.
  → «Finance sees the agreement before the first invoice arrives.»
- **Media and content production** — a different line-up of editors, illustrators and sound
  engineers for every release, changing fast. The commissioning editor approves work in a chat and
  the approval cannot be found a month later. → «The approval sits next to the invoice.»
- **Architecture and engineering design firms** — long engagements with staged statements of work;
  each stage has its own fee and acceptance. Stage three is invoiced against the wording of stage
  one. → «Each stage has its statement of work, and each invoice points to its stage.»
- **Event organisers** — a one-off crew across countries per event: producers, technicians, hosts.
  Many people, a short deadline, agreements signed on the day and collected in a rush afterwards.
  → «The event is over — the agreements and invoices are already together.»

### Brand layer — for owned tone, not for verbatim copy
- Built for companies whose work is done by people in several countries: consultancies, agencies,
  research and production teams, product companies. Tone: «We have done this paperwork ourselves.»
- Brand promise: Example Co keeps contractor paperwork in order / **Paperwork in Order** — «and
  even if you stay with your spreadsheet, we will still show you what a complete contractor record
  looks like». «We notice the missing signature before the auditor does.»
- Three brand pillars: **Order** (every document has a place, nothing lives in a personal mailbox,
  not "good enough for now" but "findable in a year") / **Clarity** (plain rules, visible statuses,
  no small print, predictable price) / **Respect** (for the contractor's time and for the
  approver's attention; a reminder instead of a reproach).
- Archetype: **Everyman**. Competitors in this market tend to take louder archetypes (Hero,
  Outlaw, Creator); keep the plain Everyman voice and do not borrow theirs.
- Communication logic — the «Yes, but…» ladder (the substrate for article theses):
  an agreement was signed ≠ the current version is the one that was signed; the invoice was paid ≠
  the invoice was approved by the person who owns the budget; a list of contractors ≠ a document
  trail; an auditor asks for the agreement behind each invoice, not for the total; an acquirer
  wants the whole set within a week; when a manager leaves, the approvals in their mailbox leave
  with them.
- Vision: a shared record of work between a company and every independent professional it works
  with — one history that both sides can open and neither has to rebuild.
- Editorial balance for output: ~70% corporate / operational / practical, ~30% marketing polish.
- «Enterprise procurement» is NOT a positioning vertical — do not lead with it.
- The template library is framed as part of the workspace, not as a standalone legal product.
