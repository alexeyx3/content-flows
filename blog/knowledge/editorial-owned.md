## Rules (verbatim-critical)

### Contractor lifecycle framing
- The engagement is primary. Approval of the invoice is the final stage of a complete lifecycle, not the whole product.
- Page flows reflect the full lifecycle order: invitation → agreement from a template → e-signature → statement of work → invoice submitted → approval → export to accounting software.

### Documentation language
- Preferred: agreements · statements of work · documents · supporting documents · document trail · approval history · records a checker can open.
- "Contract" may be used, but: do not over-use; prefer "agreement" where the product is described; especially avoid contract-drafting language (clauses, legal wording, enforceability) when it could read as legal advice from Example Co.

### Tone balance
- **70% corporate / operational / practical · 30% marketing polish.**
- Required: calm · expert · trust-building · empathetic to the pain of finance and operations teams and of founders (chasing approvals, retyping invoices, the fear of a document request nobody can answer).
- Forbidden: hype · legal-guarantee tone · salesy · conversational or hedged phrasing about Example Co · vague/fuzzy.
- **Critical rule:** never sell speed. No "instant", "same-day" or "in minutes" next to Example Co; at most "in a few steps".

### Audience-specific messaging
- Founders: "Out of your inbox" · "One place for every contractor's paperwork" · "Get back to the work itself" (instead of chasing signatures).
- Head of finance: "An agreement behind every invoice" · "A named approver" · "A document trail per contractor".
- Both audiences: use finance-operations and operations-director language, not legal or banking language.

### Template library positioning
- Frame it as a logical part of the workspace — the place where an agreement starts — not as a standalone legal product.
- Connect: the template library produces the agreement → the agreement anchors the invoices and their approval (one record, not two unrelated features).

### Visual content brief rules (for content managers briefing designers)
- Product screenshots on the site must illustrate the document routine: a contractor record · the list of signed agreements · agreement status · an invoice waiting for approval.
- Forbidden as primary illustration: a generic stock photo of a handshake · a screenshot of a contract with readable personal data · an e-signature certificate styled like an official seal or stamp.

## Notes / context
- The lexical and visual limits exist because readers, reviewers and partners read seals, «certified» labels, speed promises and legal guarantees as legal certification or as signals of a regulated legal business. The product rules allow naming invoices and approvals as functions; they do not allow presenting Example Co as a payment provider, a law firm or an employer of record.
- The copywriter skill enforces format/tone/grammar/compliance checks at generation time; this card keeps the editorial logic that is not in `positioning.md`.
