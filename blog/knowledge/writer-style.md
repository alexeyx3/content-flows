# Write human prose on the first pass

Use this compact guide while drafting. It is a generation guide, not a second editing workflow.
Facts still come only from the job's `evidence.md`; current positioning and blog rules override
every example here.

## Shared rules for EN and RU

- Write the answer first. Start a section with its concrete conclusion, not a broad observation.
- Give each paragraph one job. Remove a sentence if it merely restates the previous sentence,
  table row, heading, or list item.
- Prefer concrete actors and actions to abstractions: who does what, under which condition, and why
  it matters to the reader.
- Use as many examples or list items as the subject requires. Do not force ideas into groups of
  three or make every paragraph and card syntactically symmetrical.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length naturally. Use paragraph breaks where the idea changes; never
  split or merge prose merely to hit a word-count target.
- Do not narrate the research, verification, SERP, brief, evidence IDs, or writing instructions.
- Avoid generic scene-setting, inflated significance, vague praise, fake conversational turns,
  unsupported attribution, and conclusions that only summarize the preceding section.
- A repeated fact is allowed only when it performs a genuinely different job. Otherwise keep its
  strongest occurrence.
- Tables carry scan-friendly facts. Prose below them interprets those facts and must not repeat the
  table row by row.
- If evidence is insufficient, omit or qualify the claim and report the gap. Never fill space with
  generic prose.
- Do not use or infer a word target. Finish a section when every required coverage point is
  explained at useful depth, without padding or repetition.
- Quotation typography follows language: EN `“…”` with nested `‘…’`; RU `«…»` with nested
  `„…“`. Straight double quotes are not publication typography.

## English tells to avoid

- Empty intensifiers: `seamlessly`, `effortlessly`, `robust`, `comprehensive`, `cutting-edge`,
  `streamlined`, `holistic`, `game-changing`, `best-in-class`.
- Passive agency: repeated `helps you`, `allows you to`, `enables you to`, `empowers you to`.
  Prefer the actual actor and action.
- Formulae: `not only ... but also`, `It's worth noting`, `In summary`, `This means that`,
  `In today's landscape`, `delve into`, `here's the thing`.
- Rhetorical questions at the start of sections and repeated `X, not Y` contrasts.
- Identical three-part lists, parallel card prose, and every heading built from the same template.

## Russian tells to avoid

- Bureaucratic connectives: `является`, `данный`, `представляет собой`, `выступает в качестве`.
- Empty formulae: `широкий спектр`, `ряд преимуществ`, `важно отметить`, `следует подчеркнуть`,
  `таким образом`, `на сегодняшний день`, `в современном мире`.
- Passive agency: `позволяет`, `даёт возможность`, `обеспечивает возможность`, when a direct action
  can be named.
- Chains of verbal nouns, participial tails of significance and unnecessary anglicisms.
- Quotation marks around ordinary words, constant `не A, а B` constructions and a long dash in
  almost every sentence.
- Forced triples, identically built cards and a mechanical summary at the end of a section.

## Final self-check before handing off the complete draft

Read the assembled article once for repetition and rhythm. Fix only actual defects; do not shorten
complete sections to make the article look tighter. Confirm that every planned section is present,
every factual claim is within evidence, and no instruction or research-process language reached the
reader.
